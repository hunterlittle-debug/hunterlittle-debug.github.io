Overview

The project

NC State SolarPack is a student team building a street-legal solar car to race in the Formula Sun Grand Prix (FSGP). The car runs on a 400 V battery pack, and the battery box is what keeps the driver safe if something goes wrong with it.

On the Structures team I helped design the box and pick the firewall materials. I also updated our old SolidWorks models so they match the car as it is now, and I spot welded cells together to build the modules.

Right now the build is on hold while we wait for parts and go into winter break.

What it had to do

  • Hold the high-voltage pack and keep it walled off from the driver
  • Pass the FSGP race rules for battery boxes
  • Fit the current car
  • Hold cells wired in series into high-voltage modules
  • Protect the driver first. The main job is keeping a fire away from the cockpit, not cooling the cells
  • Keep heat away from the car's electronics

Decisions

Design choices

The main calls I made and why.

01

Picking the firewall material

We went with ceramic blankets sandwiched between other layers.

Why: First we looked at epoxies and resin layups with fire-resistant additives mixed in. Then we tried welding blankets. We ended up with ceramic blankets because they did the best job of protecting the driver. We still haven't decided on the outer layers, and whatever we pick has to pass the FSGP race rules.

02

Wiring the cells in series

The lithium-ion cells are wired in series into modules.

Why: Wiring cells in series adds their voltages together, which is how we get to the 400 V the car needs.

03

Spot welding the connections

I spot welded nickel and copper strips onto the cells to connect them.

Why: Spot welding is quick and doesn't heat up the cell much, which matters because lithium-ion cells can be damaged by too much heat.
