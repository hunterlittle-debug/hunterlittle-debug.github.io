Custom CPAP Tubing Bracket

A hook-on bracket I designed in FreeCAD and 3D printed in PLA to hold and route CPAP tubing for a family member, eliminating tubing interference and making the machine easier to use.

Overview

The problem

CPAP tubing tends to get in the way: it drapes, tangles, and pulls on the mask. A family member needed a simple fixture that would hold the tube in a better position and route it out of the way.

I designed a structural bracket in FreeCAD, 3D printed it in PLA on a Creality printer, and installed it. It eliminated the tubing interference and made the setup easier to use day to day.

The bracket hooks over the headboard, and a tubed channel on the other end holds the CPAP hose. It needs no tools or screws to install.

Goals & requirements

  • Match the channel radius to the CPAP hose
  • Route the tubing away from where it caused interference
  • Hook securely onto the headboard with no tools or fasteners
  • Hold the hose without damaging the delicate tubing
  • Printable on a home Creality printer in PLA

Engineering decisions

Design choices

The key decisions I made, the alternatives I weighed, and why I picked what I did.

01

FreeCAD for the design

Modeled the bracket in FreeCAD.

Why: I was on a tight budget, and FreeCAD was the free option. Learning it also taught me parametric modeling, so I could change a dimension and regenerate the part between test fits.

02

PLA on an FDM printer

Printed in PLA on a Creality Ender-3 Pro in about 2 hours.

Why: PLA prints accurately and is stiff enough for a light-load bracket. The short print time made it practical to iterate quickly.

03

Bracket geometry and mounting

A hook that grips the headboard on one end and a tubed channel that holds the hose on the other, refined over six versions.

Why: Each version solved one problem: first getting a secure hold on the headboard, then matching the channel radius to the hose, then making sure it held the hose without damaging it. The last version was a thinner, lighter travel model.

Reflection

What I learned

Six versions taught me that good design comes from testing on the real thing. Each print showed me something a measurement alone didn't, like how tightly the hook needed to grip or how much clearance the soft tubing needed. It also showed me the value of designing for the user: the travel version came from thinking about how the bracket would really be used.
